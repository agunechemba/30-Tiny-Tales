Chapter 15

The Alphabet Soup

Strings and Text Processing

🍜

In the alphabet soup there lived a string named “hello”. It wasn’t a word so much as a row of letter-beads, threaded in a line: h, e, l, l, o.

The string could tell you its length (five beads). It could show you a bead by its index (bead 1 is “e”). It could turn itself loud and shouty (“HELLO!”) or whispery (“hello”).

One day two strings met — “cup” and “cake” — and when they hugged, they became “cupcake”. That’s joining. Another day a string was told, “Find the ‘l’,” and it proudly pointed to bead 2.

“Text is just a row of letters,” said the spoon, “and every program that talks to people is really just moving letters around.”

Big Idea

A string is text, stored as a row of characters. You can measure it, slice it, change its case, search inside it, and join strings together.

Try It!

Write your name. Count its length. Now write it in capitals. Now write it backwards, one letter at a time.
