Chapter 14

The Train of Cupcakes

Collections — Lists and Arrays

🚂

Baker Bea made a train out of cake boxes. Each carriage held exactly one cupcake, and each carriage had a number painted on the side. Carriage 0 held vanilla. Carriage 1 held chocolate. Carriage 2 held strawberry.

“Why does the first one have a 0?” asked her helper. “Because computers start counting at zero!” said Bea. “The number on the carriage is the index. Whatever is inside is the element.”

She counted the carriages: three. “That’s the length of the train.” When a new cupcake arrived, she hitched on another carriage at the end. When one was eaten, the carriages after it shuffled along, keeping their order.

A whole train of things, kept in order and numbered, is called a list (or an array). Bea could find the first one, the last one, or every single one in a loop.

Big Idea

A list holds many values in order. Each item is an element, its position is the index (starting at 0), and the number of items is the length.

Try It!

Write a list of five friends. Who is at index 0? At index 4? What is the length of the list?
