Chapter 9

If It Rains

Conditionals and Branching

🌦️

Every morning, Jo stood at the front door and looked at the sky. If it was raining, she took her umbrella. If it wasn’t, she took her sunglasses. Either way, she went to school.

“You’re doing a conditional,” said her mum. “Your day splits into two paths, like a fork in a road. If the sky is rainy, go this way. Else, go that way.”

Jo liked that. She started noticing forks everywhere. At the traffic light: if it is green, walk; else, wait. In her game: if the score is over 100, you win; else, keep playing. And sometimes there were more than two roads — if it’s raining, umbrella; else if it’s snowing, boots; else, sunglasses.

“The computer isn’t clever,” said Mum. “It just checks the question, then takes the right road.”

Big Idea

A conditional checks something true or false and chooses a path: if this, do that; else, do something different. Choosing a path is called branching.

Try It!

Write an if/else for a traffic light. Then add a third path with “else if” for a flashing yellow light.
