Chapter 1

The Very Big Puzzle

Computational Thinking

🧩

Maya opened the box and out tumbled one thousand puzzle pieces. “A thousand!” she gasped. “I will never finish this. Not ever.”

Grandma sat down beside her. “Don’t look at the whole pile. First, find all the flat edge pieces.” So Maya did — and slowly the enormous pile turned into a neat little frame. Decomposition: one big job, broken into smaller jobs.

Then Maya noticed something. Blue pieces were sky. Green pieces were trees. She sorted them into little piles without even trying. That was pattern recognition.

“Now,” said Grandma, “do we need every single blade of grass? Or just the shape of the trees?” Maya grinned and put the boring bits aside. That was abstraction — keeping only what matters.

Last of all, Maya made a plan: edges first, then sky, then trees, then the wobbly bits in the middle. Step, step, step. That was an algorithm. By bedtime the puzzle was finished — and Maya had learned to think like a computer.

Big Idea

Computational thinking is four little habits: break it up, spot the patterns, keep only what matters, and write the steps in order.

Try It!

Pick a big job, like “tidy my room”. Break it into four smaller jobs. Find one pattern. Then write five steps, in order, that anyone could follow.
