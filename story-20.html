Chapter 20

The Calabash Inside the Calabash

Recursion

🏺

Aisha opened the big calabash and found a smaller calabash inside. She opened that one and found a smaller calabash still. And inside that one — another.

“How many are there?” she asked. Grandma said, “Open one, and ask the calabash inside. It will ask the calabash inside it, and so on.” Aisha laughed. “But that never stops!”

“It stops,” said Grandma, “because the smallest calabash doesn’t open. That’s the base case — the one that simply answers ‘one’ and goes no deeper.”

So Aisha counted: the big calabash said “one plus what’s inside me”, the next said the same, all the way down until the tiny solid calabash said “one”. Then the answers came rushing back up. A function that calls itself is called recursion — and it always needs a base case to stop it.

Big Idea

Recursion is when a function calls itself on a smaller version of the problem. Every recursion needs a base case so it knows when to stop.

Try It!

Say a countdown out loud: “3 — then countdown from 2 — then countdown from 1 — then countdown from 0 — stop!” Which part was the base case?
