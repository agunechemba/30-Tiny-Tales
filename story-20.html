Chapter 20

The Doll Inside the Doll

Recursion

🪆

Ana opened the wooden doll and found a smaller doll inside. She opened that one and found a smaller doll still. And inside that one — another.

“How many are there?” she asked. Grandma said, “Open one, and ask the doll inside. It will ask the doll inside it, and so on.” Ana laughed. “But that never stops!”

“It stops,” said Grandma, “because the smallest doll doesn’t open. That’s the base case — the one that simply answers ‘one’ and goes no deeper.”

So Ana counted: the big doll said “one plus what’s inside me”, the next said the same, all the way down until the tiny solid doll said “one”. Then the answers came rushing back up. A function that calls itself is called recursion — and it always needs a base case to stop it.

Big Idea

Recursion is when a function calls itself on a smaller version of the problem. Every recursion needs a base case so it knows when to stop.

Try It!

Say a countdown out loud: “3 — then countdown from 2 — then countdown from 1 — then countdown from 0 — stop!” Which part was the base case?
