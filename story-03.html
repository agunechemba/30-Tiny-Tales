Chapter 3

Speaking Computer

Code, Statement, Syntax

🐉

High on a hill lived a small dragon who spoke only in code. “Hello,” said Nia. The dragon blinked and said nothing.

So Nia learned the dragon’s language. Every line she spoke was one complete thought, and the dragon understood it as a statement. Say “roar()” and the dragon roars. Say “fly(3)” and the dragon flies three laps. Each statement is one tiny instruction, written in code.

But the dragon was fussy about grammar. Leave off a bracket and the dragon hiccuped smoke. Spell a word wrong and the dragon sneezed. That fussy grammar has a name: syntax.

“Why so strict?” asked Nia. “Because,” said the dragon, “if I guess what you mean, I might do something you never meant at all.”

Big Idea

Code is written in statements — one complete instruction each. Syntax is the grammar: the rules that keep the meaning clear.

Try It!

Here is a broken sentence: “the cat sat the mat on”. Fix the word order so it makes sense. Congratulations — you just fixed syntax!
