Chapter 3

Speaking Computer

Code, Statement, Syntax

🐢

High on a hill lived a small tortoise who spoke only in code. “Hello,” said Ada. The tortoise blinked and said nothing.

So Ada learned the tortoise’s language. Every line she spoke was one complete thought, and the tortoise understood it as a statement. Say “hide()” and the tortoise pulls into its shell. Say “walk(3)” and the tortoise walks three steps. Each statement is one tiny instruction, written in code.

But the tortoise was fussy about grammar. Leave off a bracket and the tortoise hiccuped dust. Spell a word wrong and the tortoise sneezed. That fussy grammar has a name: syntax.

“Why so strict?” asked Ada. “Because,” said the tortoise, “if I guess what you mean, I might do something you never meant at all.”

Big Idea

Code is written in statements — one complete instruction each. Syntax is the grammar: the rules that keep the meaning clear.

Try It!

Here is a broken sentence: “the cat sat the mat on”. Fix the word order so it makes sense. Congratulations — you just fixed syntax!
