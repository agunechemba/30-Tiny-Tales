Chapter 21

The Cookie Cutter Family

Object-Oriented Basics

🍪

In the kitchen drawer lived a star-shaped cookie cutter. It wasn’t a cookie. It was the idea of a cookie — a class. Press it into dough and you get a real star cookie: an object.

Each cookie had its own properties: one was red, one was yellow, one had a missing corner. Each cookie could also do things — it could be decorated, or eaten. Things an object can do are its methods.

One day a new cutter appeared: a star with a little hat on top. It was made from the star cutter and just added one extra thing. “That’s inheritance,” said the big cutter proudly. “It got everything from me, and then a hat.”

So: one cutter, many cookies. Same recipe, each one its own little self.

Big Idea

A class is a blueprint; an object is one thing made from it. Objects have properties (what they are) and methods (what they do). Inheritance lets a new class build on an old one.

Try It!

Design a class called Pet. What properties does it have (name, colour, legs)? What methods (speak, eat, sleep)? Now make two different objects from it.
