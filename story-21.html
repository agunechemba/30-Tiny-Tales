Chapter 21

The Chin-Chin Cutter Family

Object-Oriented Basics

🥟

In the kitchen drawer lived a star-shaped chin-chin cutter. It wasn’t a chin-chin. It was the idea of a chin-chin — a class. Press it into dough and you get a real star chin-chin: an object.

Each chin-chin had its own properties: one was golden, one was brown, one had a missing corner. Each chin-chin could also do things — it could be decorated, or eaten. Things an object can do are its methods.

One day a new cutter appeared: a star with a little cap on top. It was made from the star cutter and just added one extra thing. “That’s inheritance,” said the big cutter proudly. “It got everything from me, and then a cap.”

So: one cutter, many chin-chin. Same recipe, each one its own little self.

Big Idea

A class is a blueprint; an object is one thing made from it. Objects have properties (what they are) and methods (what they do). Inheritance lets a new class build on an old one.

Try It!

Design a class called Pet. What properties does it have (name, colour, legs)? What methods (speak, eat, sleep)? Now make two different objects from it.
