Chapter 19

The Lost Marble

Algorithms — Searching, Sorting, Efficiency

🔍

A marble was hiding in one of one hundred numbered boxes. “Guess,” said Grandpa. “Box 1? No. Box 2? No.” It might take ninety-nine guesses.

“Let me try,” said Ravi. “Is it in boxes 1 to 50?” No. “51 to 75?” No. “76 to 88?” Yes! Each question halved the boxes. Ravi found the marble in seven guesses. That’s binary search — an algorithm that’s fast because it throws away half the work each time.

Then Grandpa spilled a box of numbered cards. Ravi picked them up one at a time and slid each card into its proper place in a neat row. Slow, but tidy. “That’s a sorting algorithm,” said Grandpa. “There are faster ones, but they all do the same job: put things in order.”

“So an algorithm is a recipe,” said Ravi. “And efficiency is how few steps the recipe takes.” “Exactly,” said Grandpa, “and the short recipe leaves more time for biscuits.”

Big Idea

An algorithm is a step-by-step way to solve a problem. Searching finds one item; sorting puts items in order. Efficiency is about doing it in as few steps as possible.

Try It!

Play guess-the-number from 1 to 100, halving each time. Can you always win in seven guesses? Try it with a friend.
