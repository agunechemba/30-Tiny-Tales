Chapter 17

The Borrowed Toolbox

Modules, Libraries and APIs

🧰

Nell wanted to build a birdhouse. She started by making her own hammer out of a rock and a stick. It took all morning, and it was a terrible hammer.

“Why not use the toolbox?” asked her neighbour, opening a big red box. Inside were hammers, saws and screwdrivers that clever people had already made. “A library is a toolbox of code that someone else built. You just borrow a tool and get on with your birdhouse.”

Later, Nell needed wood. She didn’t chop down a tree herself — she went to the timber yard, asked the person at the counter, and got exactly the plank she wanted. “That’s an API,” said her neighbour. “A friendly counter. You ask in the right way, you get an answer back. You don’t need to know how the sawmill works.”

Nell finished the birdhouse before lunch — and it had a proper roof, too.

Big Idea

A library is code someone else wrote that you can use. An API is the agreed way to ask for something and get an answer back — no need to build it yourself.
