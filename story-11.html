Chapter 11

The Tester's Tea Party

Testing

🫖

Temi had made a beautiful set of clay teacups for the tea party. “They’re done!” she said. “How do you know?” asked her aunt. Temi blinked. She hadn’t actually checked.

So Temi became a tester. She poured in hot tea. She poured in cold tea. She filled one to the very top, right to the brim. She held one upside down (a bad idea, but useful to know). She even gave one to the goat.

One cup leaked. One handle snapped. Temi wrote each problem down and fixed it, then tested all over again.

“Testing is comparing what should happen with what actually happens,” said her aunt. “Test the normal things, and then test the strange things — the empty cup, the enormous cup, the cup that gets dropped.”

Big Idea

Testing means checking your work against what you expected. Good testers try the normal cases, the edge cases and the silly cases.

Try It!

Imagine a machine that adds two numbers. What should it do with 0 + 0? With 99 + 1? With −5 + 3? Write down what you expect, then check.
