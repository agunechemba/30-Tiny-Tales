Chapter 13

When the Bell Rings

Events and Event Handlers

🔔

At the front door of the shop hung a little bell. The bell didn’t ring by itself. It rang when someone opened the door — and when it rang, the shopkeeper looked up and said, “Hello!”

“You see,” said the shopkeeper, “the ringing is an event. It’s a thing that happens. And what I do next — look up and say hello — is the event handler. It’s my rule: when this happens, do that.”

Programs are full of little bells. A mouse click. A key being pressed. A finger tapping a screen. A timer going off. The program waits, quietly, until one of them rings.

Then it runs the handler: the button turns blue, the character jumps, the music starts. Waiting for something to happen is called being event-driven.

Big Idea

An event is something that happens — a click, a key press, a timer. An event handler is the set of instructions that runs when it does.

Try It!

Name three events in a game you like and what happens when each one occurs. For example: when the player touches a coin → add one point.
